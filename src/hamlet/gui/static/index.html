The idea in one paragraph

A spin chain's excitation spectrum depends on its exchange couplings. HamLeT simulates many chains with known couplings, trains a model to invert that mapping, and applies it to your measured dI/dV. Because the model is trained for one specific physical setup, every model carries a contract — chain length, bias window, observable, parameter ranges — and the package refuses to apply a model outside it rather than returning a confident wrong answer.

The usual order

  1. My data — check the measurement is readable and complete.
  2. Can I reuse a model? — if yes, skip straight to the last step.
  3. Train a model — build one for your system. Hours.
  4. Get my couplings — apply the model and get the report.

Measuring DMI is the exception: it is a sample-design question that has to be settled on the DMI sample design page before any data is taken.

DMI sample design

DMI along z is exactly unmeasurable in a conventional chain — a symmetry hides it, verified to one part in 1013. So measuring it is a question of sample design, answered before any data is taken. Describe the chain you can build and the arrangements you could put on it; this ranks them by whether they can expose DMI at all.

  1. 1 · The chain you can build

    The exchange scale is the gauge-invariant sqrt(J1_xy² + D_z²) — the number a zero-field measurement already reports. The screen builds two chains that share it and differ only in how much of it is DMI, then asks whether your measurement could tell them apart. What matters is the ratio, not the DMI alone.

  3. 3 · The arrangements you could build

    One card per candidate sample. Click the sites you would put impurities on — they are numbered from 0. Transverse anisotropy on the impurities is the mechanism that breaks the symmetry hiding Dz; a transverse field does it too. Neither is monotonic, so screen the values you can actually make rather than assuming bigger is better.

  4. 4 · Screen them

    The symmetry check is free and rules out hopeless designs immediately — no simulation, exact. The full screening simulates a gauge pair per surviving candidate, about a minute each, and reports how strongly each design imprints DMI on the spectra.

Running and finished work

Long runs happen in the background. Their output appears here as it is produced; you can leave this page and come back.

A run can be stopped at any time. Stopping is cooperative: it finishes the chain, trial or candidate it is on, so nothing is left half-written and simulations already produced are kept for the next run to resume from.
