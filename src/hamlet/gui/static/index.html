The idea in one paragraph

A spin chain's excitation spectrum depends on its exchange couplings. HamLeT simulates many chains with known couplings, trains a model to invert that mapping, and applies it to your measured dI/dV. Because the model is trained for one specific physical setup, every model carries a contract — chain length, bias window, observable, parameter ranges — and the package refuses to apply a model outside it rather than returning a confident wrong answer.

Look at a measurement

Can I reuse an existing model?

Compares your measurement against each published model's contract and answers reuse / retrain / regenerate — listing the reason a model was rejected, so you can see exactly what does not match.

The cutoff is the upper bias limit you choose to analyse. It is part of the contract and is never substituted: a model trained to 20 meV is not applied to a 60 meV window.

DMI sample design

DMI along z is exactly unmeasurable in a conventional chain — a symmetry hides it, verified to one part in 1013. So measuring it is a question of sample design, answered before any data is taken. This ranks candidate impurity arrangements by whether they can expose DMI at all.

The symmetry check is free and rules out hopeless designs immediately. Running the screening simulates a gauge pair per surviving candidate, about a minute each. Start from examples/dmi_screening.yaml.
